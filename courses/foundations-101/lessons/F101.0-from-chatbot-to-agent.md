# F101.0 — From Chatbot to Agent: Give AI a Job to Finish

**Duration:** 3 minutes 47 seconds  
**Audience assumption:** The learner has used AI mainly for questions, drafting, or not at all  
**Key concept:** Agent-ready work starts with a contained job, a source, boundaries, a deliverable, and a check—not merely a question.  
**Learner outcome:** Turn one chatbot-style question into an agent-ready task brief and complete a read-only planning action.

## A-roll script

(0:00–0:08) | Series Introduction

Welcome to the Analytics AI Enablement education series. Today in Foundations, we focus on the shift from chatbot to agent.

(0:08–1:13) | The Gap and the Core Distinction

For the past few years, many of us have used AI in a simple Q&A loop: ask a question, get a wall of text, then process it manually.

That habit is creating a widening gap between people who use AI only as a chatbot and people who know how to delegate work to modern agentic tools such as Claude Code.

A Slack survey of more than 20,000 knowledge workers across 15 countries found that people working with agents were 72% more likely to say they felt “very productive.”

People who did not use agents reported spending nearly 40% more time on administrative work than agent users. That does not prove agents caused the difference, but it makes the opportunity worth understanding.

A capable agentic system does more than answer a question. It can gather project context, use tools across multiple steps, ask for feedback and approval, run checks, and keep moving a job toward a defined finish line.

That requires more than a good prompt. Later, we will define the model, tools, context, loop, and harness that make a system agentic. First, we will make the work ready for that system.

(1:13–1:48) | Closing the Gap

The goal of this miniseries is to help you understand both the capabilities and the limits of today’s AI, then apply that understanding to your day-to-day work.

Your tool stack doesn’t matter. You might write SQL and Python all day, live in complex Excel workbooks, or maintain reporting documentation in Google Docs.

The practical courses later in the series address more in-depth analysis work. Here, we are building the foundation for using agents safely.

The point is to find where AI can reduce manual work and help you generate more value.

(1:48–2:53) | What We’ll Cover and the Thinking Shift

Across these lessons, we’ll break down the practical mechanics: writing precise prompts, managing context, using tools and the Model Context Protocol (MCP), and choosing the right model for each task.

More importantly, we’ll focus on how to think about work in the modern AI era.

With agents, your bottleneck is no longer only the time it takes to write code, clean data, or draft documentation.

The real leverage shifts to decision-making and evaluation: knowing what to delegate, what to keep human, and how to verify that an agent’s work is complete and accurate.

(2:53–3:38) | The Miniseries Promise

The series follows four steps: frame one contained task, produce a separate artifact, verify it against evidence, and understand the system—including its capabilities and hard limits.

By the end, you should be ready to identify one part of your own work where AI can help, test it safely, and evaluate the result.


Let’s get started.

## Immediate exercise and feedback loop

Rewrite one chatbot-style question as an agent-ready task brief and request a plan without changes. Check whether the plan names the correct source, deliverable, boundary, and evidence. Revise any missing element before moving on.

## Boundary

Agency increases the system's ability to act; it does not establish approval, correctness, or accountability. Begin with approved, read-only, reversible work.
