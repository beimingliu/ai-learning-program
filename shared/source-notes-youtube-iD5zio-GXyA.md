## Opening structure

- **00:00–01:47 — Relatable stuckness:** Begins with a familiar reaction to feeling behind: adding another habit, system, goal, or optimization. The speaker includes himself in the pattern so the viewer is not treated as the problem.
- **01:47–02:20 — Promise and open loop:** Previews the mechanism, why the intuitive response backfires, and a practical action later in the video.
- **02:20–04:45 — Concrete mechanism:** Uses background computer processes as a metaphor for unfinished commitments consuming attention. Reframes low energy as overload rather than laziness.
- **04:45–06:18 — Consequence:** Competing priorities encourage easy, completable activity while important work remains unfinished.
- **06:18–08:49 — Objection and reframe:** Addresses the fear that doing less means being less serious. Contrasts visible, performative effort with focused work that compounds.
- **08:49–10:00 — Immediate exercise:** Moves from argument to an inventory: list commitments, identify what was done consistently, then identify what produced a real result.

## Adaptation for Foundations 101

- Name the learner's fear of being behind frequent AI users without confirming that they are deficient.
- Include the instructor and product design in the explanation: chat was the first behavior most tools taught.
- Replace “add more goals” with “collect more prompts, models, and tools.”
- Make the counterintuitive shift: progress comes from delegating one bounded, checkable job—not from having more AI conversations.
- Contrast a disappearing chatbot answer with a durable, verified artifact or workflow.
- Preserve a short open loop: the next lesson names the reusable task-brief structure.
- End with a low-risk action: convert one question into an agent task and request a read-only plan.

## Tone guardrails

- Use productive tension, not catastrophe or job-loss threats.
- Do not imply that chatbot use is foolish; it is a reasonable first interaction and still useful for questions.
- Avoid status comparisons, attention-span praise, or claims that “serious” users behave one way.
- Give the learner a reachable next move rather than an expanding list of tools to master.
